Battery Draining After Installing a Car Stereo or Amp?

By BassDrive · 1 November 2026

If your battery goes flat overnight after a new stereo or amplifier was fitted, the installation is the first suspect. The good news is that it is usually a wiring mistake, not a faulty battery.

Common causes

  • Swapped power wires. The yellow (constant) and red (ignition) wires reversed, or both connected to constant power, keep the unit awake all night.
  • Amplifier remote wire on constant power. The amp should turn on only when the head unit tells it to. If its remote wire is on a permanent feed, it never switches off.
  • Always-on accessories. Dash cams, cameras and extra modules wired to constant power.
  • A short or bad ground. A wire touching metal can drain the battery slowly or blow fuses.

How to test for a parasitic draw

  1. Switch everything off, close the doors and wait 15 to 20 minutes so the car's modules go to sleep.
  2. Disconnect the negative battery terminal and connect a multimeter (set to amps) between the terminal and the battery post.
  3. A healthy car draws very little, typically under about 50 milliamps.
  4. If the reading is high, pull fuses one at a time. When the reading drops, you have found the circuit.

Do not turn the key or open doors while the meter is connected. A high current can blow the meter's fuse.

How to fix it

Correct the wiring so the head unit's red wire goes to an ignition-switched feed and its yellow wire to constant power. Make sure the amplifier remote wire comes from the head unit. Use properly sized power cable with an inline fuse near the battery.

When to call a technician

If you cannot find the circuit, or the draw keeps coming back, a technician with the right tools can trace it fast and prevent a dead battery or electrical damage.
