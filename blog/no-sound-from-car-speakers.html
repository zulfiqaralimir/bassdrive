No Sound From Your Car Speakers? Causes and Fixes

By BassDrive · 1 November 2026

When your car speakers go quiet, the cause is often simple. Here is how to find it, starting with the easiest checks.

1. Check the basics

Make sure the volume is up, mute is off and the correct source is selected (radio, Bluetooth, USB). Then check the fader and balance, because a setting pushed fully to one side can silence the other speakers.

2. If you have an amplifier, look at its light

Most amplifiers show a power light. A red protect light means the amplifier shut itself off. Common causes are a shorted speaker wire, a bad ground, or overheating. Switch everything off, fix the fault, then power on again.

3. Check the remote turn-on wire

An amplifier needs a remote signal (usually a blue wire) from the head unit. If it is loose, the amplifier never turns on and all sound is lost.

4. Inspect the speaker wires

Look for wires that have come loose at the speaker or the head unit, or that are pinched in a door hinge. One dead speaker usually means a broken wire or a blown speaker on that side.

5. Test the speaker

Disconnect the speaker and touch a 1.5 volt AA battery to its terminals for a moment. You should hear or see the cone move. With a multimeter on resistance, a normal 4 ohm speaker reads roughly 3 to 4 ohms. An open reading means the coil is burnt.

6. Check the ground

A poor ground on the head unit or amplifier causes weak, noisy or missing sound. Clean the contact point down to bare metal.

When to call a technician

If the amplifier keeps entering protect mode, a speaker tests open, or the head unit has power but no output, a technician can find the fault quickly and avoid damage from guessing.
