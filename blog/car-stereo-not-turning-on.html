Car Stereo Not Turning On? 7 Checks Before You Visit a Shop

By BassDrive · 1 November 2026

A car stereo that will not turn on is one of the most common car audio problems in Pakistan, and it is often a cheap fix. Work through these seven checks before you pay for a new head unit.

Safety first. Switch the ignition off and disconnect the negative battery terminal before touching any wiring.

1. Check the fuse

Most head units have a fuse (usually 10 to 15 amp) on the back panel. The car also has an audio or radio fuse in the fuse box. A blown fuse looks broken or dark inside. If a new fuse blows again at once, there is a short circuit and you should stop and get it traced.

2. Check the wiring harness plug

Pull the unit out and make sure the harness plug is pushed in fully. Loose or corroded plugs cause a stereo that works only sometimes.

3. Test the constant power wire

The yellow wire should show about 12 volts at all times, even with the key out. Use a multimeter. No voltage means a blown fuse or broken wire.

4. Test the ignition (ACC) wire

The red wire should show 12 volts when the key is in ACC or ON, and zero when it is off.

5. Check the ground

The black wire must touch clean bare metal. A rusty or painted ground point stops the unit from starting. Test for continuity between the black wire and the car body.

6. Look for a security code

Some factory and branded units lock after the battery is disconnected and ask for a code. Check the manual or the card that came with the car.

7. Try a reset

Many units have a tiny reset hole on the front panel. Press it with a pin for a few seconds. This clears most software freezes.

When to call a technician

Get help if fuses keep blowing, you smell burning, there is no voltage on the power wires, or the unit got wet. A wiring fault left alone can damage the unit or the car's electrical system.
